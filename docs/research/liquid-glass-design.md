# Apple Liquid Glass: design ethos and engineering model

Research note for the launcher's glass-material work. Researched 2026-10-15.

Method: every Apple claim below was read from the primary page or session transcript linked beside it. The developer.apple.com pages are JavaScript-rendered, so the text was read from Apple's own JSON documentation feed (`developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/...`) and the transcript HTML of each WWDC25 session. Session numbers were verified: 219 Meet Liquid Glass, 356 Get to know the new design system, 323 Build a SwiftUI app with the new design, 284 Build a UIKit app with the new design, 310 Build an AppKit app with the new design. Quotes are verbatim; timestamps are the transcript's. Secondary sources are marked **[secondary]**. Anything I infer is marked **[inference]**.

## Summary

- Apple frames Liquid Glass as a "digital meta-material" that "dynamically bends and shapes light", not as a simulation of physical glass. Its job is to form one functional layer for controls and navigation that floats above content and defers to it.
- Lensing is the primary identity: "Whereas previous materials scattered light, this new set of materials dynamically bends, shapes, and concentrates light in real time." This is refraction, not only blur.
- Glass is explicitly not for content. Navigation and controls only. No glass on glass. Regular and Clear are never mixed.
- Legibility is handled by adaptation, not fixed styling. The material, its shadows and the symbols on it adapt continuously to what is behind. Small elements flip light/dark; large ones (menus, sidebars) adapt but do not flip.
- Size changes the material. Larger glass is more opaque, with deeper shadows, stronger lensing and softer scattering. Apple states this directly.
- Clear glass has no adaptive behaviour and needs a dimming layer. The HIG gives a number: a dark dimming layer at 35% opacity when the content under it is bright.
- Glass samples an area larger than itself and cannot sample other glass. Nearby glass must therefore share a container, which gives a shared sampling region, a uniform appearance, one sampling pass and the ability to merge like droplets.
- Highlights come from light sources in a simulated environment, move on interaction (for example lock and unlock), and "in some cases" respond to device motion. Apple gives no direction, strength or per-size scaling for the rim light.
- Accessibility is built into the material: Reduce Transparency makes it "frostier", Increase Contrast makes it mostly black or white with a contrasting border, Reduce Motion lowers effect intensity and disables elasticity.
- The public API is small: one variant (regular, clear, identity), tint, interactive, a shape, a container with a merge distance, and IDs and unions for morphing. Rendering internals are not exposed.

## 1. Design ethos and principles

**Why Apple made it.** Apple's press release says Liquid Glass "combines the optical qualities of glass with a fluidity only Apple can achieve", and describes "greater harmony between hardware, software, and content" ([Apple Newsroom, 2025-06-09](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/)). Session 219 places it in a lineage: it "builds on learnings from all the way from the Aqua user interface of Mac OS X, through to the realtime blurs of iOS 7, to the fluidity of iPhone X, the flexibility of the Dynamic Island, and the immersive interface of visionOS" ([219, 0:07](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Session 356 calls it "the most extensive software design update we've made, reshaping the relationship between interface and content through a brand new set of heuristics" ([356, 0:38](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)).

**The thought process Apple describes.**

- Not a physical copy. "rather than trying to simply recreate a material from the physical world, Liquid Glass is a new digital meta-material that dynamically bends and shapes light. And simultaneously, it behaves and moves organically in a manner that feels more like a lightweight liquid" ([219, 0:38](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Harmony with hardware. "Liquid glass complements the evolution of Apple product design, as screens have gotten more rounded and immersive. It's represented in the UI in rounded, floating forms that nest neatly in the rounded curves of modern devices." The shapes "are designed to relate to the natural geometry of our fingers so they feel friendly to touch interaction" ([219, 1:10-1:41](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Shapes follow concentricity: fixed, capsule (radius is half the height) and concentric (parent radius minus padding) ([356, 3:17](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)).
- Content first. "Its primary goal is to remain visually clear, deferring to the content underneath. But it's also constantly, subtly changing to ensure legibility and to maintain clear separation from the content layer" ([219, 5:54](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). The overview page: it is "a distinct functional layer for controls and navigation elements... to help bring focus to the underlying content" ([Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/TechnologyOverviews/adopting-liquid-glass)).
- Functional layer. "Liquid Glass defines a new functional layer in the UI, floating above your content to bring structure and clarity, without ever stealing focus" ([356, 6:20](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)). Before, controls "would blend into the background when the interface was at rest. Liquid Glass helps lift them" ([356, 8:25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)).
- Quiet at rest, alive on touch. "Elements can even lift up into Liquid Glass temporarily... This lets the resting state stay visually quiet, while it comes to life on touch." The lens also lets you "precisely observe the value underneath it", for example a slider thumb ([219, 3:51-4:22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Visuals and motion designed as one. "From its foundation, both the visuals AND motion of LiquidGlass were designed as one" ([219, 2:47](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Hierarchy without decoration. Apple tells developers to strip custom bar backgrounds and borders: "hierarchy should be expressed through layout and grouping" ([356, 8:25-8:55](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)). The HIG says to use the material "sparingly", because "Liquid Glass seeks to bring attention to the underlying content" ([HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)).

**Actionable:** the stated goal is separation without opacity. A glass surface should read as a distinct lightweight layer through light behaviour (edge, lensing, shadow), not through fill. Keep it off content, quiet at rest, and reserve emphasis for interaction.

## 2. Layering

**One floating layer above content.** "Liquid Glass forms a distinct functional layer for controls and navigation elements — like tab bars and sidebars — that floats above the content layer"; content may "scroll and peek through from beneath these elements" ([HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). Navigation lives in "the topmost layer of the interface"; Apple asks for a navigation structure "distinct from the content you provide" ([Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/TechnologyOverviews/adopting-liquid-glass)).

**Why not on content.** HIG: "Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer... including it in the content layer can result in unnecessary complexity and a confusing visual hierarchy. Instead, use Standard materials for elements in the content layer." The exception is transient controls such as sliders and toggles, which take on glass "when a person activates it". Session 219's example: a table view as glass "would make it compete with other elements and muddy the hierarchy" ([219, 12:41-13:13](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).

**Glass on glass.** "always avoid glass on glass. Stacking Liquid Glass elements on top of each other can quickly make the interface feel cluttered and confusing. When placing elements on top of Liquid Glass, avoid applying the material to both layers. Instead, use fills, transparency, and vibrancy for the top elements to make them feel like a thin overlay that is part of the material" ([219, 13:13](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Apple's UIKit example removes Maps' floating glass buttons when a sheet expands, "to keep the illusion of a single floating layer of glass intact" ([284, 20:02](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/)). Also: "avoid overcrowding or layering Liquid Glass elements on top of each other" ([Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/TechnologyOverviews/adopting-liquid-glass)).

**Separation from scrolling content.** Scroll edge effects "work in concert with Liquid Glass to maintain that crucial separation between the UI and content layers". Content "gently dissolves into the background"; when glass flips dark, "the effect intelligently switches to apply a subtle dimming instead". A hard style is used for pinned accessory views ([219, 9:00-10:03](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). They are "not decorative. They don't block or darken like overlays"; apply one per view, never stack soft and hard ([356, 11:28](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)). Controls "sit on top of a system material, not directly on content. Without that separation, contrast can suffer" ([356, 10:58](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)). In steady states, "avoid intersections between content and Liquid Glass" ([219, 17:54](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).

**Grouping and containers.**

- Why containers exist is a rendering fact: "The glass material reflects and refracts lights, picking colors from nearby content. This effect is achieved by sampling content from an area larger than itself. However, glass can not sample other glass, so having nearby glass elements in different containers will result in inconsistent behavior. Using a glass container allows these elements to share their sampling region" ([323, 19:51](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)). AppKit adds: sharing means it "only needs one sampling pass for the entire group", and adaptation "is shared across the grouped elements" ([310, 19:26-20:30](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/310/)).
- `GlassEffectContainer` "combines multiple Liquid Glass shapes into a single shape that can morph individual shapes into one another"; "As shapes near one another, their paths start to blend into one another. The higher the spacing, the sooner blending begins" ([GlassEffectContainer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glasseffectcontainer)).
- UIKit: "As long as there is space between them, they appear as two separate views. Only if they get closer, they start merging like small droplets of water." `UIGlassContainerEffect` "does more than just enabling animations. It enforces a uniform adaptation" ([284, 23:39-24:40](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/)).
- Toolbar grouping: items sharing a function share one glass pill; text buttons and prominent buttons get separate glass; do not group symbols with text ([356, 8:55-9:56](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/), [284, 7:28](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/)).

**Morphing.** "Liquid Glass dynamically morphs between the controls in each context. This maintains the concept of having a singular floating plane that the controls live on." Menus: "the bubble simply pops open to reveal the content" ([219, 4:22-4:54](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Glass appears and disappears by "gradually modulating the light bending and lensing" rather than fading ([219, 2:47](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). API behaviour is in section 7.

**Actionable:** treat glass as one plane. Draw all glass surfaces that can touch or neighbour in a single pass over a shared backdrop sample, never glass-sampling-glass, and put content on top as fills or vibrancy rather than as more glass.

## 3. Vibrancy and luminance adaptation

- Layered adaptation: "unlike previous materials that had a fixed light or dark appearance, each layer continuously adapts based on what's behind it. As text scrolls underneath, shadows become more prominent... The amount of tint and the dynamic range shift to always ensure buttons remain legible, while letting as much of the content through as possible. And when needed, it can also independently switch between light and dark" ([219, 6:24](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Flip rule: "Small elements like navbars and tabbars... flip from light to dark based on the background... Bigger elements, like menus or sidebars also adapt based on context, but they don't flip from light to dark. Their surface area is too big and transitions like these would be distracting." Symbols and glyphs "flip from light to dark and vice versa, mirroring the glass's behavior to maximize contrast" ([219, 15:16-15:47](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). HIG: symbols and text "follow a monochromatic color scheme, becoming darker when the underlying content is light, and lighter when it's dark. Liquid Glass appears more opaque in larger elements like sidebars" ([HIG: Color, Liquid Glass color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)).
- UIKit states the size rule operationally: "Glass adapts the appearance based on its size. A larger size is more opaque. A smaller size is clearer, and switches between light and dark mode automatically, to increase contrast." Labels "automatically become vibrant, based on its textColor" ([284, 21:05-21:38](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/)). SwiftUI: "SwiftUI automatically uses a vibrant text color that adapts to maintain legibility against colorful backgrounds" ([323, 18:14](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)). AppKit passes the light/dark decision to content through `NSAppearance` ([310, 2:10-2:43](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/310/)).
- Regular: "blurs and adjusts the luminosity of background content to maintain legibility", works "in any size, over any content and anything can be placed on top of it". Most system components use it; prefer it for text-heavy surfaces such as alerts, sidebars and popovers ([HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials), [219, 13:44](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Clear: "highly translucent", for components over media such as photos and video. "Clear, on the other hand, does not have adaptive behaviors. It is permanently more transparent... To provide enough legibility for symbols or labels, it needs a dimming layer... Without it, legibility gets noticeably worse." Use it only when (1) the element is over media-rich content, (2) the content layer is not harmed by dimming, and (3) the content above it is bold and bright. Smaller footprints can use localized dimming ([219, 14:16-14:46](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Dimming numbers: HIG says "If the underlying content is bright, consider adding a dark dimming layer of 35% opacity"; no dimming is needed over sufficiently dark content or with AVKit controls that bring their own ([HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). The `Glass.clear` doc example uses `.background(.black.opacity(0.3))` ([Glass.clear](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glass/clear)). The two numbers differ (35% versus 30%): treat both as starting points.
- Never mix Regular and Clear ([219, 13:44](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Tinting: "By default, Liquid Glass has no inherent color, and instead takes on colors from the content directly behind it" ([HIG: Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). Tint is not an overlay fill: "Selecting a color generates a range of tones that are mapped to content brightness underneath the tinted element. It draws inspiration from how colored glass works in reality: changing its hue, brightness and saturation depending on whats behind without deviating too much from the intended color." A solid fill "is completely opaque and breaks the visual character of Liquid Glass" ([219, 15:47-17:21](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Rules: tint only primary actions ("When every element is tinted, nothing stands out"); put brand colour in the content layer; "apply color to the background rather than to symbols or text" for emphasis ([HIG: Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). Tinted glass colour "automatically adapts to a vibrant version" ([284, 22:39](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/)).
- Standard materials, for the content layer under glass, keep the older rule: thicker is more opaque and better for fine text, thinner is more translucent ([HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)).

**Actionable:** drive glass appearance from a luminance estimate of the backdrop under each surface; flip small surfaces (and their glyphs) with hysteresis but never flip big ones; make tint luminance-mapped, not a flat alpha fill.

## 4. Optics

What Apple states:

- Lensing versus blur: Liquid Glass "bends, shapes, and concentrates light in real time" where "previous materials scattered light"; this gives "definition against the background content while still feeling visually grounded" ([219, 2:13](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). The press release: "reflects and refracts its surroundings" ([Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/)); the developer docs: "blurs content behind it, reflects color and light of surrounding content, and reacts to touch and pointer interactions in real time" ([Applying Liquid Glass to custom views](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views)). UIKit insists glass "is distinct from other visual effects, like UIBlurEffect" ([284, 19:31](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/)).
- Sampling: the material "samples content from an area larger than itself" ([310, 20:30](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/310/)). Colour from nearby content spills onto large surfaces: "Light from colorful content nearby can subtly spill onto its surface... the light reflects, scatters, and bleeds into the shadow as well" ([219, 7:58](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Thickness with size: "its material characteristics change to simulate a thicker, more substantial material. It casts deeper, richer shadows, has more pronounced lensing and refraction effects, and a softer scattering of light" ([219, 6:55](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Shadows are adaptive: "The element is aware of what's behind it and increases the opacity of its shadow when it is over text. Conversely, it lowers the opacity of its shadow when it is over a solid light background" ([219, 11:38](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Interaction glow: "the material illuminates from within as a form of feedback. Starting right under your fingertips, the glow spreads throughout the element and onto any Liquid Glass elements nearby" ([219, 11:38-12:08](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)). Interactive glass "scal[es], bounc[es], and shimmer[s]" ([323, 19:18](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)). Sliders "preserve momentum and stretch" ([284, 17:55](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/)). Touch gets more emphasis than a trackpad ([HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)).
- Transition: materialize by modulating light bending, not alpha ([219, 2:47](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/); `GlassEffectTransition.materialize` fades in content and animates the glass material, per [its doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glasseffecttransition/materialize)).
- Focus: when a window loses focus on Mac or iPad, "Liquid Glass shifts its appearance and visually recedes" ([219, 12:08](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Icons: system icons take "specular highlights, refraction, and translucency", and the system applies them dynamically; Apple tells designers to avoid baking in "specular highlights, drop shadows between layers, beveled edges, blurs, glows" because "custom effects are static, whereas the system supplies dynamic ones" and to "avoid soft and feathered edges" so "system-drawn highlights and shadows look best" ([HIG: App icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons)).

Not stated by Apple: any refraction formula, index of refraction, Fresnel falloff, blur radius, displacement magnitude or chromatic aberration. "Fresnel-like edge behaviour" is not an Apple term. Community shader recreations do model it **[secondary]** (a rim-weighted refraction offset from a signed-distance-field normal, plus a directional specular term, for example [fresnel_shader](https://pub.dev/packages/fresnel_shader), [oc_liquid_glass](https://pub.dev/packages/oc_liquid_glass), [archisvaze/liquid-glass](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/archisvaze/liquid-glass)). They are visual approximations, not specifications of Apple's renderer. **[inference]** Apple's wording ("more pronounced lensing and refraction" for larger elements) implies refraction strength is a function of element size or notional thickness, so a size-parameterised displacement is a sound model.

## 5. Rim light and edge highlight

**What Apple states.**

- "Liquid Glass lives inside an environment that behaves like the world around us. Light sources inside of this environment shine on the material producing highlights that respond to geometry just as you'd expect. On interactions, such as locking and unlocking your phone, these lights move in space, causing light to travel around the material, defining its silhouette. And in some cases, the lighting responds to device motion, making it feel like Liquid Glass is aware of its position in the real world" ([219, 11:04](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Press release: it "dynamically reacts to movement with specular highlights" ([Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/)).
- Highlights are shape-aware and silhouette-defining: "respond to geometry", "defining its silhouette". The icon guidance says pre-masked layers "negatively impact specular highlight effects" ([HIG: App icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons)), which confirms highlights are computed from the final shape edge.
- Interaction brightens the surface from the touch point ([219, 12:08](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Reduced Motion "decreases the intensity of some effects"; Increase Contrast adds "a contrasting border" ([219, 18:26](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).

**What Apple does not state.** I found no Apple statement of the light direction or elevation, whether the primary light is fixed in screen space or in the device frame, rim width, strength, colour, falloff, how any of these scale with shape size or corner radius, or which sensors drive the "device motion" response. The phrase "in some cases" is the whole of Apple's qualification on motion response.

**Observed, from third parties [secondary].**

- MacStories reports the Home Screen uses "the gyroscope and accelerometer to provide specular highlights" and that icon layers "adjust their reflections according to your movement" ([MacStories iOS 26 review, Home Screen and App Icons](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-26-the-macstories-review/4)).
- A search summary of Android Authority and other press says dark-mode icons show an upper-left highlight that shifts with motion, and that Low Power Mode suppresses it. I did not open or verify those articles; weak evidence.

**Inference (labelled).**

- **[inference]** A simple, defensible model is: one dominant light plus a weaker ambient floor, both producing a thin bright band along the silhouette edge facing the light, with a fainter counter-edge. Community recreations use this shape and keep an ambient floor so edges stay visible on dark backdrops ([fresnel_shader](https://pub.dev/packages/fresnel_shader) **[secondary]**).
- **[inference]** Motion response is best treated as a small rotation of the light vector driven by device tilt, with no change to geometry. Apple's phrase "lights move in space" on lock/unlock suggests the light vector can also be animated by events.
- **[inference]** Because Apple says highlights "respond to geometry", rim width should be defined in physical units and the highlight placed from the shape's edge normal, so it hugs corner curvature rather than being a rectangular border. Whether Apple scales rim width with size is unknown.

## 6. Accessibility

- Settings change the material, and apps need not do the work: "Reduced Transparency, makes Liquid Glass frostier and obscures more of the content behind it. Increased contrast, makes elements predominantly black or white and highlights them with a contrasting border and Reduced Motion decreases the intensity of some effects and disables any elastic properties for the material." These "act as modifiers on the material that change certain layers of Liquid Glass" and apply automatically to the system material ([219, 17:54-18:26](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Apps are told to test custom elements and animations under these settings; the material and morph animations "can adapt to people's needs", and a user-selectable preferred glass look also exists in Settings ([Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/TechnologyOverviews/adopting-liquid-glass), [HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). The clear and regular variants "can differ in response to certain system settings".
- Colours must supply light, dark and increased-contrast variants "to support Liquid Glass adaptivity" ([HIG: Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)).
- Reduce Motion in the HIG: reduce "automatic and repetitive animations, including zooming, scaling, and peripheral motion" ([HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)).
- Not stated: the exact blur radius, opacity or border width used under each setting.

**Actionable:** treat each setting as a modifier on parameters (more frost and opacity; black or white fill plus hard border; fewer highlight and spring effects), not as a separate theme.

**What the API shape implies.**

- The material is a parameter-free system effect. Apps pick a variant (regular, clear, identity), a tint, interactivity and a shape. There is no blur radius, refraction strength, light direction or rim knob. Apple keeps appearance an internal function of backdrop, size and context ([219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)).
- Shape is an input, because the effect is computed from the silhouette (lensing, rim, shadow). The modifier takes any `Shape`, and docs suggest rounded rectangles for larger components that "would look odd as a Capsule or Circle".
- Glass is a group-level render, not per view: containers exist for correctness (shared sampling, glass cannot sample glass), uniform adaptation, a single sampling pass, and metaball-style merging driven by one distance parameter. Apple warns that "Creating too many Liquid Glass effect containers and applying too many effects to views outside of containers can degrade performance. Limit the use of Liquid Glass effects onscreen at the same time" ([Applying Liquid Glass to custom views](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views)).
- Identity-based morphing: shapes need stable IDs to tween geometry; spacing doubles as the "can morph" threshold. A container whose spacing exceeds the stack spacing merges at rest ([same doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views)).
- `identity` is the off state used to animate glass in and out; it makes a clean API for "no glass" without re-laying out.
- Content is part of the material: the modifier "applies the foreground effects over a view", so label vibrancy and flipping are done by the effect, not by the app. Modifier order matters: apply `glassEffect` after other appearance modifiers ([same doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views)).
- Apple offers an opt-out for the whole look (`UIDesignRequiresCompatibility` in Info), showing glass is a runtime style layer ([Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/TechnologyOverviews/adopting-liquid-glass)).

## Implications for an Android glass implementation

1. Model one glass layer, not many independent blurs. Render all glass surfaces from one shared backdrop capture (a region larger than each surface), and never sample glass from glass. This matches Apple's container rule and is also the cheap path (one sampling pass).
2. Use refraction, not blur alone. Displace the backdrop sample inward along the shape's edge normal (from a signed-distance function of the actual shape), strongest near the rim, with only light frosting. Treat strength and frost as functions of element size: bigger surface means more refraction, more opacity, deeper and softer shadow. Apple states the direction; the numbers are yours to tune (**inference** for the function form).
3. Two variants only. Regular: adaptive, frosted, safe anywhere. Clear: no adaptation, needs dimming. Never mix on one screen. Use roughly 30 to 35% black dimming under Clear over bright content, localized for small surfaces.
4. Estimate backdrop luminance per surface and drive tint, shadow strength and glyph colour from it. Flip light/dark only for small surfaces (add hysteresis); big surfaces get more opacity instead and never flip.
5. Make shadow adaptive: stronger over busy or text-heavy content, weaker over flat light backgrounds.
6. Implement tint as luminance-mapped colour, not a flat alpha fill, and restrict it to primary actions.
7. Do not apply glass to content; only the navigation or control layer, plus transient controls. Put content on glass as vibrant fills, not as further glass. Keep surfaces from overlapping at rest.
8. Add a scroll-edge effect (soft fade or blur of content beneath floating glass, hard variant for dense bars) to keep glass legible over scrolling content, rather than raising glass opacity.
9. Provide a group primitive with a merge distance and stable IDs so neighbouring surfaces can melt together and morph (metaball-style field over the SDFs), plus a materialize transition that animates lensing strength instead of alpha.
10. Rim light: use a thin edge-aligned band from one dominant light plus an ambient floor, positioned from the shape normal, with width in dp. Drive the light vector from the rotation-vector sensor, low-pass filtered, with a small amplitude; disable on Reduce Motion or power-saver. All of this is inference, since Apple documents none of it.
11. Wire platform accessibility to material parameters: remove-transparency means higher frost and opacity; high contrast means black or white fill plus a hard border; remove-animations means no spring, no light motion, lower highlight intensity.
12. Optional, unsourced: a faint noise layer (about 2-3%) against banding in blurred gradients, and a modest backdrop saturation lift to offset blur washout. Apple documents neither; tune by eye and keep them out of anything presented as Apple behaviour.
13. Budget performance: cap simultaneous glass surfaces, group them, and fall back to a plain frosted layer on weak GPUs; Apple itself warns about the cost of many effects.
14. Concentric shapes: derive inner radii from container radius minus padding, and use capsules for small controls. This is the shape language Apple's material depends on.

## Gaps: what Apple doesn't say

- visionOS glass: Apple says specular highlights and shadows "reinforce its scale and position in your space" and that glass "responds dynamically to lighting" ([10076](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10076/)), but does not say speculars follow head position. A head-tracked Fresnel rim (Medium article) is unsupported by Apple on either platform.
- Rim light: direction, elevation, strength, width, colour, falloff, and scaling with size or corner radius are not documented. Whether the main light is fixed in screen space or device space is not stated. Motion response is described only as "in some cases... responds to device motion". Sensors, filtering and limits are undocumented. Third-party reports of gyroscope/accelerometer use come from press (MacStories), not Apple.
- Refraction: no index of refraction, displacement magnitude, curve or chromatic behaviour. No blur radius or saturation boost. "Fresnel" is never used by Apple.
- Thickness: size-dependent changes are described qualitatively (more opaque, deeper shadow, stronger lensing); no thresholds or curves.
- Luminance adaptation: no switching thresholds, hysteresis, or sampling method for "brightness underneath".
- Tint mapping: "a range of tones... mapped to content brightness" is not specified further.
- Dimming: two figures appear (35% in the HIG, 30% in an API example); neither is called a rule.
- Accessibility: effect of each setting is qualitative only, with no numeric parameters. Behaviour on low-power mode is not in the documentation I read.
- Rendering internals: number of passes, resolution of the sampling region, how containers batch, GPU cost. Only the user-visible consequences are given.
- The "preferred look for Liquid Glass" setting is mentioned in Apple docs without describing its modes.
- I could not retrieve developer.apple.com pages as rendered HTML; the text came from Apple's documentation JSON feed and session transcript HTML. Video content shown on screen (visuals not in the transcript) was not reviewed.

## Secondary source check: Medium (Manavkaushal, 2025)

Checked: ["The Quiet Engineering Behind Apple's Liquid Glass UI"](https://medium.com/@manavkaushal756/engineering-behind-apple-liquid-glass-ui-fb51b1d599ad) (Manavkaushal, 2025-12-27). It cites no Apple sources, and its own text is framed around visionOS ("When Apple introduced visionOS... They call it 'Glass'"; "In 2D interfaces (iOS/macOS), glass is flat. Apple's own sessions treat the two as related but distinct: session 219 lists "the immersive interface of visionOS" among the learnings Liquid Glass builds on ([219, 0:07](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)), while the HIG describes visionOS "glass" as its own "unmodifiable system-defined material" ([HIG: Materials, visionOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). That is a different API from `glassEffect`.
