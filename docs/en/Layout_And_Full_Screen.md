# Layout and full screen

This page covers where the bars stand, their sizes, the keyboard type, minimal mode and full screen.
All of it is in **Appearance**, on the card of the thing you tap (see
[Look and themes](Look_And_Themes.md#appearance)).

## Open Appearance

- From Settings.
- Hold a corner of a page and tap **Appearance**.
- Long-press the terminal and choose **Appearance**.
- From the command palette.

Each opens on that page's own card: Home's on Home, the terminal's over a window.

Home and the Workspace share one layout, so whatever you arrange lands on both; Home just leaves
out the extra keys. To give Home a layout of its own, turn on **Own layout** under More on the Home
card. Home's own layout decides which rows Home shows, in what order and on which edge, and its dock
design and Search field. The sizes stay shared: the apps row's height, the Strip row's height, the
keyboard's height and chin, and Style, Corners and Margin are the same on Home and the Workspace,
whichever one you change them from, so nothing resizes as you swipe. Portrait and landscape each
have their own layout.

## Move a bar

Every bar has a **Position** on its card. The status bar's is on the Status bar card. The apps
row, the A–Z index and the extra keys are the **Bars** list on the Dock card: tap a bar's sliders
glyph for its settings, Position among them. Where two bars share an edge, the order of the Bars
list is the order they stack in; hold a row's handle to reorder.

The terminal stays where it is and the bars arrange around it. The A–Z index can ride the apps
row.

**Bottom or above the keyboard.** Along the bottom, **Bottom** keeps the apps row or the extra keys
at the foot of the screen with the keyboard opening above them; **Above the keyboard** stands them
over it. The status bar always stands over the keyboard.

**The other orientation.** The editor always edits the orientation the phone is in, and the chip
on the editor bar says which one. Turn the phone to edit the other one: the editor stays open and
its cards turn with it.

## Hide and bring back

Turn a bar off with the eye on its card (on the Dock card, the eye on its row in the Bars list).
Hidden bars wait as small cards beside the frame: tap one to open its card, or drag it onto the
frame and let go to bring it back where it was.

**Keyboard on/off.** The keyboard's **Visible** is the keyboard's on/off switch. Off, nothing raises
it, not a tap on the terminal and not a text field, until you turn it on again. It is the same
switch as **Keyboard on/off** in the command palette, and one switch for every place and both
orientations. A swipe up from the bottom border also turns it back on. On Home it decides whether
the keyboard stays up there.

## Sizes

Each size is set per orientation.

- **Dock design**: **Design** on the Dock card, on any page: **Tray** (the default), **Beads** or
  **Strip** (a big clock and a tile for each terminal; the clock slides out at three or more
  terminals). **Search field**, under More, adds the command-palette field under the apps row (with
  Beads it sits in its own capsule under the beads).
- **Dock height**: **Height** on the Dock card. The icons grow and shrink with it.
- **Keyboard height**: **Height** on the Keyboard card.
- **Strip height**: under **Dock type** on the Dock card while the Strip is the type.
- **Bottom padding**: on the Keyboard card, the air under the last row of keys.
- **Widget grid**: **Columns** and **Rows** on the Home card. See [Widgets](Widgets.md#grid-size).

## Keyboard type

**Type** under More on the Keyboard card: docked, floating or split, for the orientation the frame
shows. **Key corners** rounds the key caps. Sizes for the floating and split keyboards are on
[Keyboard](Keyboard.md).

## Style, corners and margin

- **Docked** joins the bars into one flush glass frame, with the content as a rounded insert.
- **Floating** gives each bar its own card.
- **Corners** and **Margin** apply in both styles and to every surface; any surface's card changes
  them for all.

## Minimal mode

Minimal mode is a second saved layout for windows. It starts with only the content showing: the status bar, the
pinned apps, the A–Z index, the extra keys and the keyboard go away, and the widgets, terminal or
display take the room. Open Appearance while it is on to choose what it keeps.

- **Turn it on**: **Minimal** on the Appearance card, or hold a corner and tap the four outward
  corners. **Turn it off**: the same switch, or the same corner button, now pointing inward.
- It is one mode for every window, and it affects only windows: Home ignores it and keeps its normal
  look. Moving between pages never turns it off, and it stays on across restarts. From anywhere,
  including Home, the command palette's **Minimal on/off** action switches it.
- Everything else works as in your normal layout. Splits show all their panes, and the status bar
  and borders answer the same gestures.
- The keyboard goes down when you enter. Swipe up from the bottom border, or tap the terminal, to
  raise it.

The page border stays drawn in minimal mode, so you can still swipe along it to change place.

## Full screen

- **Full screen** on the Appearance card (also **Settings → Terminal → Text & display → Full
  screen**) hides Android's status and navigation bars. It is off by default.
- Hiding the launcher's own status bar gives its band to the content too.
- With **Wallpaper colors** on (Appearance), the padding around a pane takes the colours at its
  edge, so a full-screen program reaches the screen's edge.

## Border gestures

These work on every place and in every layout, minimal mode included:

- Swipe sideways along the top or bottom border to change place. On the left and right borders,
  hold first, then drag.
- Swipe up or down on the bottom border to open or close the keyboard.
- Swipe down or up on the top border to open or fold the status bar, while it stands along the top.

See [Learn the launcher](Learn_The_Launcher.md#move-around).
